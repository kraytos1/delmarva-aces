01Game day, start to finish

The whole broadcast runs off one phone. This is the order that works.
1
Set the lineup — /lineup.html

Batting order (9 slots), starting pitcher (picked roster-wide — he doesn't have to be in the order), and the Home / Away radio (home bats the bottom). For a dry run, tick Practice / test game — a test game is invisible everywhere on the site and never pings families.

2
Start the YouTube stream, then start scoring — /score.html

On setup, ⚡ Find live auto-fills the live video ID. Start Scoring locks clip timing to the broadcast's real start time. If you scored earlier and got interrupted, the live game pre-selects with a Resume Scoring button — mid-game reload is safe. Bringing the radar gun? Start its bridge on the laptop now too — one command, see Radar gun.

3
Put the scorebug on the stream

Paste the scorebug URL (copy it from Coach HQ) into the YoloBox as a Web URL Overlay, or into OBS as a browser source. It finds the live game by itself. Sponsors rotate beside it; between innings it plays a 3-sponsor card, then a Due Up panel with the next three hitters and their headshots.

4
Optional: the AI booth — /booth.html

Voice play-by-play that calls each play as you score it. Route its audio into the stream mix. Audition voices at /tts-demo.html.

5
Families follow along on their own

The gamecast (/game.html) shows the stream, live field, and play feed. Every page shows a live strip under the nav. Families who tapped the 🔔 bell get a push at first pitch and at the final.

6
End the game in the scorer

Final push goes out (real games only). Clips are already queued from scoring — see After the game.

First real game? Run one Practice game end-to-end first — same flow, zero consequences, delete it afterwards in Games Admin.

02The scorer

One-thumb operation, built for the bleachers. Everything is undoable; nothing needs to be perfect in the moment.

Pitches and outcomes

  • Ball / Strike / Foul are raw pitch taps. Ball four logs the walk by itself; strike three logs the K by itself. A foul with two strikes stays at two. One Undo removes the pitch and the outcome it triggered, together.
  • Outcome buttons (hits, outs, reach-on) end the at-bat and advance the order automatically. A same-button double-tap inside half a second is ignored — that's a phone bounce, not two plays.
  • Baserunner events (steal, WP/PB, balk, caught stealing, pickoffs, out advancing) live behind their own button. They never advance the batter or reset his count. Steals and caught-stealings ask who ran — that's how the kid gets SB credit.
  • Velocity: with the radar gun running (see Radar gun) the number fills itself — 📡 in the unit label means the gun set it, 📡 last means it just attached to the pitch you already tapped. No gun? Nudge or tap it before the pitch. Either way it rides on that pitch's row and feeds the scorebug, bullpen and player pages.

The helper chips (all optional, all off-ignorable)

  • Dropped 3rd — after a legal-D3K strikeout (1st open, or two outs), a chip offers Safe at 1st / Out at 1st. Either way it counts as a strikeout in the stats.
  • Base check — after a ball in play with runners on, confirms the by-the-book advance actually matched the field. The Fix sheet corrects bases, runs, and the at-bat row.
  • 🧤 Fielder tag — when the Aces record an out, tag the glove(s) in play order. Feeds the Defense filter in Highlights and each kid's reel.
  • Spray tap — after any batted ball, the field diagram arms; tap where it landed. Builds each kid's spray chart on his player page.

Mid-game management

  • ⇄ Subs: pitching change, pinch hitter / runner, position change, opponent pitcher and batters.
  • ⇄ HOME/AWAY chip in the count bar if the sides were set wrong at lineup time.
  • Skip batter (the de-emphasised button) names exactly who it will skip and asks first. The order advances by itself after every at-bat — you almost never need this.
  • Manual corrections (score, count pips, outs, bases, inning) are all undoable, and Undo always names what it's about to take back.
  • Arm care: your pitcher's live pitch count wears Pitch Smart tier colors and toasts at each rest threshold.

It's built to survive the field

  • Works offline — plays queue locally and sync when signal returns. The sync dot tells you which mode you're in.
  • Screen wake lock while scoring; pull-to-refresh and accidental-close are guarded.
  • The video panel collapses on phones (your choice is remembered); every spare pixel goes to the buttons.

03Radar gun new Sep 2026

Stalker Sport 3 Connect. The gun measures, the laptop listens, the scorer does the bookkeeping. You never type a velocity again.

Setting up

1
Mount the gun, continuous trigger on

Two positions work, and nothing changes between them. Behind the backstop, pointed at the mound. Or out in center field with you, pointed at the plate — the gun sees 300 ft, so this is a real option. Either way the beam wants the ball coming straight at it or straight away. Continuous trigger mode means nobody has to squeeze anything all game.

2
Connect it — cable or wireless, not both

USB-C cable to the laptop is the bulletproof option. Bluetooth needs no pairing and no cable — but reach is roughly 30–100 ft, and while a cable is plugged in the gun talks to the cable only. The 300 ft is how far the gun can see, not how far it can talk: in center field, keep the laptop beside the gun.

3
Start the bridge — the Android tablet (no laptop), or the laptop

Tablet, the everyday way: on the Samsung (or any Android phone) open /radar.html in Chrome, tap 📡 Connect gun and choose the Stalker. Leave it near the gun on your hotspot with the screen on; every reading shows big on screen as it lands. Your iPhone stays the scorer — nothing changes there. (iPhone and iPad can't run this page; Safari has no Bluetooth access.)

Laptop, the wired way: python C:\Users\c_boo\OneDrive\Desktop\AcesAnalytics\radar_bridge.py. Picks USB if the cable's in, otherwise Bluetooth; reconnects by itself. Start it from a plain terminal, one copy only — two bridges fight over the gun's single Bluetooth link. Either bridge queues readings through dead spots and sends them when signal returns.

What you'll see while scoring

  • The velo box fills itself. 📡 in the unit label = the gun set it, ready for the pitch you're about to tap.
  • 📡 last = the reading landed after you'd already tapped the pitch, so it attached to that pitch retroactively. Your thumb is faster than the gun's round trip — this is the normal case, and it's correct.
  • The box empties after every pitch. Blank means the gun missed one — honest, not a repeat of the last number.
  • Tap a number yourself and yours wins for that pitch. A gun reading older than 20 s (warm-up throws, pickoffs) is never glued to a pitch.
  • The other team's pitcher gets gunned too — those numbers feed the scouting pages.
  • On the stream, the scorebug's pitching line gets the gun's number about half a second after the ball leaves the hand — before the mitt pops — over a live push from the bridge, and flashes 💥 exit velo as the ball comes off the bat. That's a display-only fast lane; the number that reaches the stats is the held, filtered one. If the push connection drops the bug falls back to a slower 2–3 s refresh, never blank. ?radar=off on the scorebug URL turns radar off entirely.

Exit velocity

  • A pitch is in the air for about half a second; a batted ball comes right after it. The bridge splits each burst on time — release region = pitch, anything after the flight = the ball off the bat — so every ball in play gets an exit velocity, for free. A 💥 Exit velo toast confirms each one. (This gun doesn't report direction, so position doesn't matter — backstop or center field, same rules.)
  • It attaches to the at-bat when you pick the result, and shows up on the kid's player page as Exit Velocity · In-Game: max, average, 70+ mph count, and his three hardest-hit balls.
  • Built-in filters: the catcher's throw back to the mound is ignored (no pitch in that burst), and a foul straight back at the gun can't overwrite the pitch speed.
If it's quiet: gun not advertising — it's asleep or off, pull the trigger once. db offline, N queued — wifi; it'll flush. Nothing printing on Bluetooth while it's on the charger? The gun is talking to that cable — unplug it, or run the cable to the laptop instead. No reading at all on a live pitch? Aim: the beam wants the ball coming straight at it or straight away.

09How plays count audited Aug 2026

The scoring engine was audited play-by-play against the rulebook (71 checks). The short version:
  • At-bats: walks, HBP, sacrifices, and baserunner events are not ABs. Errors, fielder's choices, and dropped-third strikes are.
  • Strikeouts: dropped 3rd counts as a K — batter and pitcher — whether or not the kid reached.
  • RBI: credited on hits, sacs, and bases-loaded walks; never on errors, double plays, or runner events.
  • Forces & timing: fielder's choice models the real force chain; a run never scores on a play whose out ends the inning.
  • Steals: SB/CS credit goes to the runner you picked, not the batter at the plate.
  • OBP uses the rulebook denominator (AB+BB+HBP+SF).
  • The one honest proxy: "R" is runs that scored on a kid's plays, not runs he personally scored — individual runner crossings aren't tracked.
